# Notes on the two CERN R2E papers on neutrons at CLEAR

Both papers were found by Logan (Nov 2026). They were read in full from the open-access versions (both are CC BY 4.0). The numbers below are the papers' own, with page or table references.

## [L22] Lerner et al. 2022, the THz dump

G. Lerner et al., "Analysis of the photoneutron field near the THz dump of the CLEAR accelerator at CERN with SEU measurements and simulations," *IEEE Trans. Nucl. Sci.* 69(7), 1541–1548 (2022). doi:10.1109/TNS.2022.3157404. Open-access accepted version: jyx.jyu.fi.

- **Chip:** 32 Mbit ISSI SRAMs. **Table II gives σ_sat = 1.4e-14, E0 = 0.01, W = 14.05, S = 0.82**, the same Weibull fit we use. The thermal cross-section is 3.2e-15 cm².
- **Target:** the THz beam dump, which is a 35 cm graphite core, then 20 cm iron, then 20 cm concrete. This is not our target.
- **Beam (Table I):** 205 MeV, 30 nC per train, 10 Hz, giving 6.75e15 electrons per hour (high intensity). The test itself ran at 12 nC.
- **Six positions (Table III).** Observed/predicted SEU ratio, without and with a B4C lid:
  - **POS1**, in front of the dump, about 10 cm from the beam axis: 1.0 / 1.3
  - **POS2**, side of the dump: 1.4 / 1.9
  - **POS3**, behind the dump on the beam axis: **2.1 / 6.3**
  - **POS4**, top of the dump: 0.9 / 1.6
  - **POS5**, 30 cm off axis: 1.0 / 1.6
  - **POS6**, 30 cm off axis: no data without the lid / 1.3
- **Measured rates** run from 15 to 483 SEU/h per chip at high intensity, depending on position.
- **Expected accuracy** of the FLUKA predictions is about ±50% (Section IV-B). Measured and predicted agree within a factor of 2, except at POS3.
- **Photon-driven SEUs:** **POS3, on the beam axis behind the dump.** There the flux of photons above 10 MeV is 2e11 cm⁻²/h, about 1,000 times the neutron (HEH-eq) flux. With the B4C lid the measured rate is 6.3 times the neutron-only prediction. The photon SEU cross-section of the ISSI chip is unknown.
- **Thermal neutrons:** a boron carbide (B4C) lid removes them. At POS2 the rate drops from 282 to 79 SEU/h with the lid. The paper suggests FLUKA may slightly overestimate the thermal flux, which depends on the hydrogen content of the concrete.

## [L23] Lerner et al. 2023, an aluminium target

G. Lerner et al., "Analysis of the radiation field generated by 200-MeV electrons on a target at the CLEAR accelerator at CERN," *IEEE Trans. Nucl. Sci.* 70(8), 1572–1579 (2023). doi:10.1109/TNS.2023.3252808.

- **Chip:** Table I names **ISSI IS61WV204816BLL-10TLI**, which is exactly our reference chip. It was tested alongside two Cypress SRAMs.
- **Target:** an aluminium alloy (6082) cylinder, **15 cm long**. Radius 5 cm in Run 1, 2.5 cm in Runs 2–3. Run 4 used a 30 cm long target (p. 1575).
- **SRAM positions:** two panels, lateral and top, both **10 cm from the beam axis** (p. 1575).
- **Shielding:** Run 3 adds **5 cm of lead** between the target and the lateral panel. Run 4 (latch-up measurements) adds 10 cm of lead and iron.
- **Beam:** about 205 MeV, 10 Hz. Results are scaled to 30 nC per train, i.e. 6.75e15 electrons per hour (p. 1576).
- **Neutron spectrum:** extends above 100 MeV but **peaks around 1 MeV** (Fig. 5, p. 1575). The thermal peak, from the concrete, is small and was neglected.
- **SEU rates:** **every SRAM measured more than 10³ SEU/h**, and the Cypress-65 nm reached almost 10⁴ (Fig. 8, p. 1577).
- **Agreement with FLUKA:** measured rates show **"mild excesses (below 40%)"** over the predictions (p. 1577). This is a one-sided excess, not ±40%.
- **Lead shielding:** strongly reduces the dose rate, with much smaller changes in the SEU rate (p. 1577). The paper doesn't give a percentage, so read the rates off Fig. 8.
- **Dose:** about 1 kGy/h on the panels, 300 kGy/h downstream on the beam axis (p. 1576).
- **No pile-up:** the neutron flux is too low for pile-up within a single train (p. 1576).

## What this means for BURST

1. **Novelty.** CERN has already measured upset rates in our exact chip at CLEAR. BURST must not claim the rate as new. What's new is the clustering (multi-cell upsets, MCUs) and its effect on error correction; neither paper studies it. Cite them as proof that the setup works and that the chip responds as predicted.
2. **Our rate is plausible but not validated.** Our estimate is about 1,120 flips/h per chip at 30 cm behind 2 cm tungsten + 10 cm lead. L23 measured more than 1,000/h at 10 cm from aluminium. Tungsten makes more neutrons per electron than aluminium, but we're three times farther away, so the two can't be compared directly. **Validation task (Prithwijit):** rebuild the L23 Run 2/3 geometry in Geant4 and fold with Logan's σ(E). If the result is within about 40% of their measured ISSI rate, our model is validated.
3. **Photons at our position:** probably negligible. The photon problem in L22 was on the beam axis behind the dump. The off-axis positions 30 cm away (POS5 and POS6) agreed with the neutron-only prediction within 1.0–1.6. Our position is at 90°, 30 cm away, behind lead. **The direct-beam arm is the exception:** it sits in the electron and photon field, which is why we use Coronetti's electron cross-section there.
4. **Thermal neutrons:** our 10 cm polyethylene setup gets 75% of its upsets from the thermal term. L22 shows that FLUKA thermal predictions are uncertain and that a B4C lid separates the thermal contribution in a measurement. **Idea for Logan:** add a run with a B4C or cadmium cover, so the thermal-neutron share of our flips is measured rather than assumed.
5. **Accuracy to quote:** CERN's own predictions for this chip at CLEAR are good to a factor of about 1.5–2. Our feasibility numbers should carry a similar uncertainty.
